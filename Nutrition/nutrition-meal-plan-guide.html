Diet Guideline Guardrails

  • Keto: Keep net carbs commonly below 20-50 g/day; emphasize sodium, magnesium, potassium-rich foods, and fiber from low-carb vegetables.
  • Vegan: Build each day around legumes, tofu/tempeh, seitan if gluten is tolerated, soy milk, pea/rice protein, nuts/seeds, and B12-fortified foods or supplementation referral.
  • Vegetarian: Use eggs/dairy only if tolerated and preferred; otherwise follow vegan protein structure.
  • Gluten-free: Avoid wheat, barley, rye, and cross-contact risk; use rice, potatoes, corn, oats labeled gluten-free, quinoa, beans, fruit.
  • Dairy-free: Use fortified soy/pea milk, lactose-free only if intolerance rather than allergy, tofu, fish, poultry, legumes, or protein powders matched to restrictions.

The Hand Method

No scale needed — your hand scales with your body, so it stays personal.

  • Palm = one protein portion (≈ 20–25 g protein): chicken, fish, lean beef, tofu.
  • Cupped hand = one carb portion (≈ 20–25 g carbs): rice, potato, oats, fruit.
  • Thumb = one fat portion (≈ 8–10 g fat): oils, nut butter, cheese, avocado.
  • Fist = one vegetable portion (≈ 1 cup): greens, broccoli, peppers.

BMI is a screen for body size, not composition — muscular clients read "high" while lean. Use it alongside the mirror, the log, and performance, never alone. Calorie estimates (Mifflin-St Jeor) are starting points: hold each target 2 weeks, watch the scale trend, then adjust ±100–200 kcal.

1-2 hoursSmaller carb + protein meal; keep fat and fiber moderate if the client gets GI distress.
15-45 minutesEasy carbs if needed: banana, applesauce, sports drink, rice cake, dates, or low-fiber bar.
Keto clientsUse protein + electrolytes; consider targeted carbs only if compatible with their plan and performance needs.

Post-Workout Recovery

WindowBest fit
Within 0-2 hoursProtein feeding plus carbs, especially if training again within 24 hours.
Protein20-40 g high-quality protein, or roughly 0.25-0.4 g/kg per feeding.
CarbsPrioritize carbs after hard lifting, intervals, long endurance, or two-a-day training.
FluidsReplace sweat losses; if body weight is measured, drink about 1.25-1.5 L per kg lost over the next few hours.

During Training

  • Under 60 minutes: water is usually enough unless heat, heavy sweating, or low pre-workout intake changes the situation.
  • 60-150 minutes: consider 30-60 g carbohydrate per hour for endurance or high-output sessions.
  • Over 150 minutes: advanced endurance clients may practice 60-90 g carbohydrate per hour from mixed carb sources, with sodium and fluid matched to sweat rate.

Key Criteria To Add

  1. Calories or portion method: Decide whether the program uses calorie targets, hand portions, or both.
  2. Body metrics: Current weight, height, age, sex, body composition estimate, waist, and goal timeline.
  3. Training load: Session type, duration, intensity, weekly volume, sport demands, and step count.
  4. Medical screen: Diabetes, kidney disease, hypertension, GI disorders, pregnancy, eating-disorder history, medications, supplements.
  5. Readiness and logistics: Budget, cooking skill, kitchen access, schedule, meal frequency, grocery access, cultural/religious foods.
  6. Adherence score: Hunger, energy, cravings, sleep, digestion, performance, mood, and weekly compliance.
  7. Progress rules: How often to adjust calories, carbs, hydration, and sodium based on weight trend and performance.
  8. Restaurant/travel rules: Simple ordering templates and emergency allergy instructions.
  9. Supplement policy: Creatine, caffeine, protein powder, electrolytes, vitamin D/B12/iron referrals when appropriate.
  10. Escalation criteria: When the coach refers out to a physician, allergist, or registered dietitian.
